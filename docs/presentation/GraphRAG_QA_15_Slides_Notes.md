# GraphRAG-QA - 15-slide presentation: speaker notes

## Slide 1 - Title

Good morning. We are Dhruv Shah and Manasvi Saxena, and this project was done under Dr. Sujoy Chatterjee at the School of Computer Science, UPES Dehradun. Our project is GraphRAG-QA: a question-answering system that answers multi-hop questions over several documents. It builds an evidence graph of passages and entities, finds reasoning chains through it, reranks evidence with a graph attention network, and answers only with cited evidence. The small graph on the right is our running example, Q1, which needs two documents joined by the entity Geoffrey Hinton. Headline numbers: on 300 held-out HotpotQA questions, evidence recall rises from 0.818 to 0.910.

## Slide 2 - Problem and motivation

The problem. A multi-hop question needs facts from two or more passages, often in different documents. In Q1, the Nobel PDF says the laureate from Toronto is Geoffrey Hinton, and only the Hinton profile says where he got his PhD. The second passage shares almost no words with the question, so a retriever that scores passages one by one ranks it low. HotpotQA [2] was built around exactly this bridge pattern. Standard RAG [1] also answers fluently when the evidence is missing, and gives no audit trail. Our idea: connect passages through the entities they share, and rank passages as a group rather than one at a time.

## Slide 3 - Objectives and contributions

Six contributions. One, an evidence graph built automatically from uploaded documents with spaCy, with four node types and six edge types. Two, multi-hop evidence chains found as simple paths from the question, plus a Personalized PageRank heuristic. Three, a Graph Attention Network reranker trained on HotpotQA. Four, grounded answers: Flan-T5 answers from the evidence, citations are checked, a confidence score is computed from measurable signals, and the system abstains when evidence is weak. Five, a complete application. Six, a reproducible evaluation with a significance test.

## Slide 4 - Related work

Related work. RAG [1] retrieves once from a flat dense index. Multi-hop dense retrieval [4] reformulates the query after the first hop. PathRetriever [5] follows Wikipedia hyperlinks, which ordinary uploaded documents do not have. HGN [6] reasons with a GNN, but over paragraphs that are already given and with a large encoder. GraphRAG [7] and HippoRAG [8] build their graphs with an LLM, which is costly at indexing time. We build the graph cheaply with spaCy, keep PageRank as in HippoRAG, add a small learned GAT reranker, and make every answer auditable with citations, a confidence score and abstention.

## Slide 5 - System architecture

Architecture. Four layers. The React single-page app has seven pages, including the Graph Explorer built with React Flow. It talks REST/JSON to a FastAPI service. A composition-root container wires the processing services: ingestion, chunking, embeddings, entity extraction, the evidence graph, chain finding, the two rerankers, answer generation, and citation and confidence checks. Storage is local: SQLite for documents, passages and history, a FAISS index, the graph as JSON or GraphML, and the model files including the GNN checkpoint. The Wikipedia API is used only in open-domain mode.

## Slide 6 - End-to-end pipeline

The pipeline in eleven steps. Indexing happens once per document: extract text, cut it into 400-token windows with 80 tokens of overlap, embed each passage with MiniLM, extract entities and verb relations with spaCy, then build the FAISS index, the BM25 index and the evidence graph. Answering happens per question: hybrid retrieval, a question subgraph, chain search, reranking, generation and verification. The grey chips are measured timings on the five demo documents; the graph steps take a few milliseconds each. Generation dominates the cost.

## Slide 7 - The evidence graph

The evidence graph. Four node types: document, passage, entity and question. Six edge types, listed on the right with the rule that creates each one and its weight in Personalized PageRank. The key edge is CONNECTS_TO: two passages are linked when they share a bridge entity, such as Geoffrey Hinton in Q1. To stop hub entities from linking everything, dates are never bridges, and an entity must appear in at most max(4, min(40, 0.25 N)) passages. The drawing is a simplified view of Q1; the real Q1 subgraph has 58 nodes and 201 edges.

## Slide 8 - Scoring: hybrid retrieval, chains and the graph heuristic

Three scores. First, hybrid retrieval mixes cosine similarity of MiniLM embeddings with BM25, normalised by the best BM25 score for the question; alpha is 0.7. Second, every chain is a simple path from the question node; its score rewards relevance, anchored on the first passage, IDF-weighted coverage of question terms and bridge specificity, with a small penalty per extra hop. Third, the graph heuristic adds Personalized PageRank restarted at the question, and chain support. When the GAT is selected, it replaces the heuristic score and the chains are re-scored with the GAT probabilities.

## Slide 9 - GAT reranker: architecture and training

The GNN. Each node gets 392 features: the passage embedding multiplied element-wise by the question embedding, plus eight scalar relevance features. A linear layer projects them to 64 dimensions and adds a node-type embedding. Two GATConv layers with four heads use the edge type as an edge feature, with residual connections. An MLP scores the concatenation of the input and output representations, and a wide linear path from the eight scalars goes straight to the logit, which kept the strongest signals from being diluted. It was trained with binary cross-entropy on 3,000 HotpotQA question graphs, with three negatives per positive, half of them hard. Early stopping on validation MRR chose epoch 13.

## Slide 10 - Grounded answers, confidence and abstention

Answering safely. Flan-T5-base reads the top five evidence passages with a cautious prompt that allows 'unanswerable'. If it abstains, a plain reading-comprehension pass may still answer, but only at half support and only if grounded. Then three gates: the best passage must reach cosine 0.22 and term coverage 0.30; the entities named in the question must appear in the evidence; and the confidence must reach 0.30. Confidence is computed from measurable signals, never by a language model. Q1 passes with 0.67. Q11, about the 2030 World Cup, is refused: the model proposed South Korea, but the evidence never mentions the World Cup, so entity grounding rejects it.

## Slide 11 - Experimental setup and main results

Results. We evaluated five strategies on 300 HotpotQA questions from the official dev split, distractor setting, so each question is answered over its own ten paragraphs, two gold and eight distractors. The GNN was trained on separate training questions. The configuration is top-k 4, two hops, and k = 2 for precision. Both graph strategies beat all retrieval-only strategies. Evidence recall, the share of gold paragraphs that reach the generator, rises from 0.818 for hybrid to 0.898 with the heuristic and 0.910 with the GNN. Exact match rises from 0.313 to 0.377 and 0.373. MRR is about 0.93 for all hybrid-based strategies: the first gold paragraph is almost always ranked first, and the gain comes from finding the second, bridge paragraph.

## Slide 12 - Statistical significance and error analysis

Is the gain real? A paired bootstrap over the 300 questions, 10,000 resamples. Filled dots are significant: the 95% interval excludes zero. Both graph strategies beat hybrid retrieval significantly on exact match, precision at 2 and evidence recall. The GNN versus the heuristic is a statistical tie on every metric. So our claim is: graph reranking clearly helps, and a learned GAT matches a hand-tuned heuristic without hand-set weights. The gain comes from bridge questions. Comparison questions stay weak for every strategy; Q10 in our demo set is an example where the GNN puts the wrong passage first.

## Slide 13 - Live demo and efficiency

The demo. The Question Answering page shows the answer, its citations, the confidence breakdown and the time of every stage. For the live demo we ask Q1, then Q4 twice: hybrid retrieval answers the Nobel Prize in Physics, which is wrong, and the GNN answers the ACM A.M. On the right are the measured per-question costs: the whole graph part, subgraph, chains and GAT, takes about 19 milliseconds. Generation dominates.

## Slide 14 - Conclusions, limitations and future work

To conclude. Evidence graphs help multi-hop question answering: both graph strategies significantly improve evidence recall and exact match over retrieval alone. A small GAT matches the tuned heuristic without hand-set weights. And every answer is auditable. Limitations: small CPU models, weak comparison questions, a 300-question sample with a two-hop setting, no significant GNN advantage over the heuristic, and the Wikipedia mode is shown only qualitatively. Future work: stronger readers with citation-constrained generation, better extraction, question decomposition for comparisons, and larger-scale training and evaluation.
